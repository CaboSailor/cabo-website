Driving from Los Angeles to Cabo San Lucas is one of the great North American road trips &mdash; 1,850 km (1,150 miles) of desert, coastline, and wide-open Baja. It&rsquo;s not a quick trip, but if you have three days and a sense of adventure, the drive down Mexico&rsquo;s Highway 1 is worth every mile.

Here&rsquo;s everything you need to plan the drive in 2026.

---

## The route at a glance

The standard route takes you from LA through Tijuana and then the full length of the Baja Peninsula on Mexico&rsquo;s Highway 1 (Carretera Transpeninsular). Plan for a minimum of three driving days &mdash; rushing it defeats the purpose.

## What you need before you go

<ul class="spot-list">
  <li><strong>Valid passport</strong> &mdash; required for the border crossing at Tijuana. Must be valid for the duration of your trip</li>
  <li><strong>FMM tourist permit</strong> &mdash; available at the border or online in advance. Technically required for stays beyond 7 days or travel south of Ensenada, though enforcement varies. Get one to be safe</li>
  <li><strong>Mexican auto insurance</strong> &mdash; your US or Canadian car insurance does <strong>not</strong> cover you in Mexico. Purchase a policy from a Mexican insurer before you cross. Policies start around $25/day. This is non-negotiable</li>
  <li><strong>TIP (Temporary Import Permit)</strong> &mdash; <strong>not required</strong> for the Baja Peninsula. Baja is a &ldquo;free zone,&rdquo; so you don&rsquo;t need a vehicle import permit</li>
  <li><strong>Vehicle prep</strong> &mdash; full tank of gas, spare tire in good condition, basic toolkit, extra water (4&ndash;8 liters per person), coolant, and a phone charger. Cell service is spotty in central Baja</li>
  <li><strong>Cash (pesos)</strong> &mdash; many gas stations and small towns are cash-only. Withdraw pesos before crossing or at an ATM in Tijuana</li>
</ul>

## Crossing the border at Tijuana

The San Ysidro border crossing is the busiest land crossing in the Western Hemisphere. Expect 30&ndash;90 minutes depending on the time and day.

<ul class="spot-list">
  <li><strong>Best time to cross</strong> &mdash; early morning (before 8 AM) on a weekday. Weekend mornings are also manageable. Avoid Friday afternoons and Sunday evenings</li>
  <li><strong>SENTRI lane</strong> &mdash; if you have a SENTRI pass, use the dedicated lanes for a 5&ndash;15 minute crossing</li>
  <li><strong>At the border</strong> &mdash; have your passport and FMM ready. The officer may ask your destination and length of stay. Be straightforward</li>
  <li><strong>Right after crossing</strong> &mdash; follow signs for the Tijuana &ndash; Ensenada toll road (Cuota 1D). It&rsquo;s well-maintained, well-signed, and avoids city traffic</li>
</ul>

## Road conditions and driving tips

Highway 1 is a two-lane road for most of the peninsula. It&rsquo;s paved and generally in decent condition, but it&rsquo;s nothing like a US interstate.

<ul class="spot-list">
  <li><strong>Two-lane road</strong> &mdash; expect slow trucks, sharp curves, and limited passing opportunities. Be patient</li>
  <li><strong>Gas stations</strong> &mdash; Pemex stations are spaced every 100&ndash;200 km, but don&rsquo;t push it. Fill up whenever you&rsquo;re below half a tank, especially between El Rosario and Guerrero Negro (the longest stretch)</li>
  <li><strong>Drive during daylight only</strong> &mdash; livestock, potholes, and unmarked speed bumps (topes) are serious hazards at night. Plan to be at your destination by sunset</li>
  <li><strong>Military checkpoints</strong> &mdash; these are normal and routine. Soldiers may ask where you&rsquo;re going, check your trunk, or wave you through. Stay calm and cooperative</li>
  <li><strong>Speed bumps (topes)</strong> &mdash; approaching every small town. Often unmarked or poorly signed. Slow down whenever you see buildings ahead</li>
  <li><strong>Roadside assistance</strong> &mdash; the &ldquo;Green Angels&rdquo; (Angeles Verdes) are a government-sponsored roadside assistance service that patrols Highway 1. Call 078 for help</li>
</ul>

## Overnight stops worth exploring

<ul class="spot-list">
  <li><strong>Ensenada</strong> &mdash; wine country, fish tacos at the Mercado Negro, and the famous La Bufadora blowhole. Worth an extra day if you have the time</li>
  <li><strong>Guerrero Negro</strong> &mdash; gateway to the gray whale calving lagoons (Jan&ndash;Mar). Boat tours to see mothers and calves up close</li>
  <li><strong>Loreto</strong> &mdash; optional detour. Historic mission town, excellent snorkeling at Isla Coronado</li>
  <li><strong>La Paz</strong> &mdash; the capital of Baja California Sur. Beautiful malec&oacute;n, stunning beaches (Balandra), and a completely different vibe from Cabo. </ul>

## Safety on the drive

The Baja Peninsula is considered safe for road trips and has been a popular driving destination for American and Canadian travelers for decades.

<div class="bg-blue-50 border border-blue-200 rounded-xl p-6 my-6">
  <p class="font-bold text-blue-800 text-lg mb-2">Safety tips for the drive</p>
  <ul class="text-blue-900 text-sm space-y-1">
    <li>&bull; <strong>Drive only during daylight hours</strong> &mdash; this is the single most important safety rule</li>
    <li>&bull; <strong>Stay on Highway 1</strong> &mdash; don&rsquo;t venture onto unmarked dirt roads unless you know where they go</li>
    <li>&bull; <strong>Keep valuables out of sight</strong> &mdash; same as any road trip anywhere</li>
    <li>&bull; <strong>Download offline maps</strong> &mdash; cell service drops out for long stretches in central Baja. Google Maps and Maps.me both support offline downloads</li>
    <li>&bull; <strong>Travel with extra water and food</strong> &mdash; some stretches between towns are 2&ndash;3 hours long</li>
  </ul>
</div>

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a vehicle permit (TIP) to drive to Cabo?**

No. The entire Baja Peninsula is a &ldquo;free zone&rdquo; for vehicles. You do not need a Temporary Import Permit (TIP). However, if you plan to take a ferry from La Paz to mainland Mexico, you will need one.

**Is it safe to drive from LA to Cabo?**

Yes. The Baja Peninsula has been a popular road trip route for decades. Stick to Highway 1, drive during the day, carry Mexican auto insurance, and use common sense. Military checkpoints are routine and nothing to worry about.

**Can I use my US car insurance in Mexico?**

No. US and Canadian auto insurance policies are not valid in Mexico. You must purchase a separate Mexican auto insurance policy before crossing the border. Several US-based companies sell these online &mdash; policies typically run $25&ndash;$50 per day depending on coverage.

**How is the road condition on Highway 1?**

Highway 1 is a paved two-lane road in generally good condition. There are occasional potholes, rough patches, and construction zones. It&rsquo;s not a highway in the American sense &mdash; expect curves, slow trucks, and small-town speed bumps. A standard car with good tires handles it fine.
