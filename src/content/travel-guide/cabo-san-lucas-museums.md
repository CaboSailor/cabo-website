Most visitors come to Cabo for the beaches and the ocean &mdash; but there&rsquo;s more to the region than sand and surf. Baja California Sur has a fascinating natural and cultural history, and a handful of well-curated museums bring it to life. Whether you need a midday escape from the heat or a rainy-day backup plan, these are the museums and cultural sites worth your time.

## Museo de Cabo San Lucas

Located on **Plaza Amelia Wilkes** in downtown Cabo, this small museum covers the natural history of the Baja Peninsula &mdash; geology, marine life, desert ecosystems, and the indigenous Pericú people who lived here before European contact.

<ul class="spot-list">
  <li><strong>What you&rsquo;ll see</strong> &mdash; fossils, whale skeletons, displays on Baja&rsquo;s desert flora and fauna, and artifacts from the Peric&uacute; culture</li>
  <li><strong>Hours</strong> &mdash; generally open Tuesday&ndash;Sunday, 10 AM&ndash;5 PM. Check locally as hours can shift</li>
  <li><strong>Entry</strong> &mdash; approximately $3 USD (50 MXN)</li>
  <li><strong>How long</strong> &mdash; 30&ndash;45 minutes is enough for most visitors</li>
  <li><strong>Kid-friendly</strong> &mdash; yes, the whale and marine life displays appeal to younger visitors</li>
</ul>

---

## Museo de Historia Natural (Natural History Museum)

On the campus of the **Universidad Aut&oacute;noma de Baja California Sur (UABCS)** in La Paz, this museum focuses on the peninsula&rsquo;s unique ecology &mdash; from deep-sea creatures of the Sea of Cortez to the cacti and reptiles of the central desert.

<ul class="spot-list">
  <li><strong>What you&rsquo;ll see</strong> &mdash; taxidermy specimens, geological samples, interactive displays on marine biology, and a section on Baja&rsquo;s endemic species</li>
  <li><strong>Hours</strong> &mdash; Monday&ndash;Friday, 9 AM&ndash;3 PM (university schedule)</li>
  <li><strong>Entry</strong> &mdash; free</li>
  <li><strong>How long</strong> &mdash; 45 minutes to 1 hour</li>
  <li><strong>Kid-friendly</strong> &mdash; very. The hands-on displays and animal specimens keep kids engaged</li>
</ul>

---

## Museo de la Ballena (Whale Museum)

The **Whale Museum** on La Paz&rsquo;s malec&oacute;n is the region&rsquo;s best museum for families. It houses full-size whale skeletons, interactive exhibits on marine mammals, and detailed information on the gray whale migration that brings thousands of whales to Baja every winter.

<ul class="spot-list">
  <li><strong>What you&rsquo;ll see</strong> &mdash; life-size whale and dolphin skeletons, baleen displays, audio recordings of whale songs, and exhibits on conservation efforts in the Sea of Cortez</li>
  <li><strong>Hours</strong> &mdash; Tuesday&ndash;Saturday, 9 AM&ndash;6 PM. Shorter hours on Sunday</li>
  <li><strong>Entry</strong> &mdash; approximately $3 USD (50 MXN). Kids under 5 free</li>
  <li><strong>How long</strong> &mdash; 1&ndash;1.5 hours</li>
  <li><strong>Kid-friendly</strong> &mdash; absolutely &mdash; this is the top pick for families. Children love the skeleton displays and the audio stations</li>
</ul>

---

## Museo Regional de Antropolog&iacute;a e Historia

La Paz&rsquo;s **Regional Anthropology and History Museum** covers 10,000 years of human history in Baja California Sur &mdash; from the cave paintings of the Sierra de San Francisco to the Spanish missions and the mining era.

<ul class="spot-list">
  <li><strong>What you&rsquo;ll see</strong> &mdash; Peric&uacute; and Guaycura artifacts, reproductions of cave paintings, colonial-era maps, and displays on the Jesuit mission system</li>
  <li><strong>Hours</strong> &mdash; Monday&ndash;Saturday, 9 AM&ndash;6 PM</li>
  <li><strong>Entry</strong> &mdash; approximately $4 USD (70 MXN)</li>
  <li><strong>How long</strong> &mdash; 1&ndash;1.5 hours</li>
  <li><strong>Kid-friendly</strong> &mdash; better for older kids (10+) who are interested in history. Younger children may find it less engaging</li>
</ul>

## Misiones Jesuitas (Jesuit Missions)

While not a museum in the traditional sense, the network of **Jesuit mission churches** scattered across Baja California Sur is one of the region&rsquo;s most important cultural treasures. The closest to Cabo is the **Misi&oacute;n de San Jos&eacute; del Cabo** (1730), whose original site is marked in downtown San Jos&eacute;. Further afield, **Misi&oacute;n de Loreto** (1697) &mdash; the first permanent Spanish settlement in all of the Californias &mdash; is beautifully preserved and worth the trip.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are there museums in Cabo San Lucas?**

Yes. The Museo de Cabo San Lucas on Plaza Amelia Wilkes is the main museum in town, covering natural history and indigenous culture. For a wider selection, La Paz (2 hours north) has three excellent museums including the Whale Museum.

**Is the Whale Museum in La Paz free?**

No, but it&rsquo;s inexpensive &mdash; approximately $3 USD (50 MXN) for adults. Children under 5 enter free. It&rsquo;s considered one of the best family-friendly museums in all of Baja California Sur.

**What is the best museum for kids?**

The Museo de la Ballena (Whale Museum) in La Paz is the clear winner for families. Full-size whale skeletons, audio whale song stations, and interactive exhibits keep children engaged for over an hour.
