Lover&rsquo;s Beach (Playa del Amor) is one of the most iconic spots in Cabo San Lucas &mdash; and one of the best places to snorkel. Tucked between the towering rock formations of Land&rsquo;s End, the beach faces the calm Sea of Cortez side, creating a sheltered, shallow snorkeling area with clear water, colorful fish, and the dramatic backdrop of the Arch just a few hundred meters away.

Here&rsquo;s everything you need to know to snorkel at Lover&rsquo;s Beach.

---

## Why Lover&rsquo;s Beach for snorkeling

<ul class="spot-list">
  <li><strong>Sheltered from the Pacific</strong> &mdash; the beach sits on the Sea of Cortez side of Land&rsquo;s End, protected from open-ocean swells</li>
  <li><strong>Shallow depth</strong> &mdash; 1.5&ndash;5 m (5&ndash;15 ft) near shore, perfect for beginners and casual snorkelers</li>
  <li><strong>Land&rsquo;s End backdrop</strong> &mdash; you&rsquo;re snorkeling next to the Arch, one of the most photographed natural landmarks in Mexico</li>
  <li><strong>Accessible marine life</strong> &mdash; the rocky bottom and boulders around Land&rsquo;s End create habitat for a wide variety of tropical fish</li>
  <li><strong>No boat traffic in the snorkel zone</strong> &mdash; the swimming area is roped off from boat traffic, making it safe and calm</li>
</ul>

---

## Marine life you&rsquo;ll see

The rocky reef around Lover&rsquo;s Beach supports a surprisingly diverse range of marine species:

<ul class="spot-list">
  <li><strong>King angelfish</strong> &mdash; the signature fish of the Sea of Cortez, with bright blue and orange coloring</li>
  <li><strong>Parrotfish</strong> &mdash; colorful, beak-like mouths, often heard crunching on coral</li>
  <li><strong>Pufferfish</strong> &mdash; spotted and porcupine varieties, usually hovering near the rocks</li>
  <li><strong>Rays</strong> &mdash; stingrays resting on sandy patches, occasional spotted eagle rays in deeper water</li>
  <li><strong>Sea turtles</strong> &mdash; green and hawksbill turtles are spotted regularly, especially in summer and fall</li>
  <li><strong>Sergeant majors</strong> &mdash; schools of small, striped fish that swarm around snorkelers</li>
  <li><strong>Moray eels</strong> &mdash; peeking out from crevices in the rocks. Look but don&rsquo;t touch</li>
</ul>

---

## How to get there

Lover&rsquo;s Beach is only accessible by water &mdash; there&rsquo;s no road or walking path.

<ul class="spot-list">
  <li><strong>Water taxi</strong> &mdash; small pangas (open boats) depart from the Cabo San Lucas marina every few minutes. Round trip costs approximately $15 USD. Agree on a return time with your driver</li>
  <li><strong>Snorkeling tour</strong> &mdash; most <a href="/shared-snorkeling/" class="text-accent-gold font-semibold hover:underline">shared snorkeling tours</a> and <a href="/private-snorkeling/" class="text-accent-gold font-semibold hover:underline">private snorkeling tours</a> include a stop at or near Lover&rsquo;s Beach as part of the route</li>
  <li><strong>Kayak or paddleboard</strong> &mdash; you can paddle from Medano Beach to Lover&rsquo;s Beach in about 30&ndash;45 minutes. Rentals available on Medano</li>
</ul>

---

## What to bring

<ul class="spot-list">
  <li><strong>Snorkel gear</strong> &mdash; bring your own or rent from shops near the marina ($10&ndash;$15/day). Tour operators provide gear on guided trips</li>
  <li><strong>Reef-safe sunscreen</strong> &mdash; chemical sunscreens damage coral and marine life. Use mineral-based (zinc oxide) formulas</li>
  <li><strong>Rash guard</strong> &mdash; sun protection and warmth in one. The water can feel cool in winter months</li>
  <li><strong>Water shoes</strong> &mdash; the beach has some rocks at the waterline, and the rocky entry points are slippery</li>
  <li><strong>Waterproof phone case</strong> &mdash; for photos. The underwater clarity at Lover&rsquo;s Beach is excellent</li>
  <li><strong>Water and snacks</strong> &mdash; there are no vendors on Lover&rsquo;s Beach. Bring everything you need</li>
</ul>

## Divorce Beach warning

<div class="bg-yellow-50 border border-yellow-200 rounded-xl p-6 my-6">
  <p class="font-bold text-yellow-800 text-lg mb-2">Do NOT swim at Divorce Beach</p>
  <p class="text-yellow-900 text-sm mb-2">Lover&rsquo;s Beach and Divorce Beach (Playa del Divorcio) are back-to-back on opposite sides of the same narrow strip of land. <strong>Divorce Beach faces the open Pacific</strong> and has:</p>
  <ul class="text-yellow-900 text-sm space-y-1">
    <li>&bull; <strong>Extremely dangerous rip currents</strong> that pull swimmers out to sea within seconds</li>
    <li>&bull; <strong>Powerful shore break</strong> that can slam you into the sand</li>
    <li>&bull; <strong>No lifeguards</strong></li>
    <li>&bull; Multiple drowning deaths over the years</li>
  </ul>
  <p class="text-yellow-900 text-sm mt-2">Walk over to Divorce Beach for photos of the Pacific &mdash; the views are stunning &mdash; but <strong>stay out of the water</strong>. Swim and snorkel only on the Lover&rsquo;s Beach (Sea of Cortez) side.</p>
</div>

## Frequently asked questions

**Is snorkeling at Lover&rsquo;s Beach safe?**

Yes &mdash; the Lover&rsquo;s Beach side faces the Sea of Cortez and is calm and sheltered. Depths are shallow (5&ndash;15 ft near shore), and the swimming area is separated from boat traffic. The key is to stay on the Lover&rsquo;s Beach side and never enter the water at Divorce Beach on the Pacific side.

**How do I get to Lover&rsquo;s Beach?**

By water only. Take a water taxi from the Cabo San Lucas marina (about $15 round trip), join a snorkeling tour that stops there, or kayak/paddleboard from Medano Beach (30&ndash;45 minutes). There&rsquo;s no walking access.

**Can kids snorkel at Lover&rsquo;s Beach?**

Yes, it&rsquo;s a good spot for older kids (8+) who are comfortable in the water. The shallow areas near shore are calm and clear. Younger children should stick to the wading zone. Always supervise closely &mdash; there are no lifeguards.

**What is the best time of day to snorkel at Lover&rsquo;s Beach?**

Morning (8&ndash;10 AM) offers the best conditions &mdash; calmer water, better visibility, fewer crowds, and more active marine life. By midday, water taxis have brought more visitors and the wind can pick up. If you&rsquo;re going independently, aim for first water taxi out.
